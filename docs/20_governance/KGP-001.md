# KGP-001: Knowledge Governance Specification

**Status**: [Validated] (The necessity of Governance) / [Reasoned Hypothesis] (The specific mechanisms)

## 1. Executive Summary: The Unified Theory of Governance Failures
Recent Phase VII execution trials produced two distinct system-level failures:
- **EXP-027 (Contradictory Knowledge)**: ACA acquired mutually exclusive categories (`Mammal` and `Fish`) without triggering clarification or contradiction resolution.
- **EXP-031 (Long-Term Continual Learning)**: ACA suffered catastrophic forgetting when updating $S_{semantic}$ across 1,000 lessons using isolated pre-eviction hooks.

**Unified Theory** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
Both experiments failed due to a singular missing architectural concept: **Knowledge Governance**. In the current ACA runtime, knowledge acquisition is treated as a terminal operation. In EXP-027, an edge was added locally. In EXP-031, a gradient step was applied locally. In both cases, local incremental updates were applied directly to a global state without a global consistency audit or historical distribution buffering. 

Learning determines *how* knowledge is acquired. Governance determines *how* it is integrated and maintained. Without governance, local updates poison the global state over time.

---

## 2. Computational Definition of Knowledge Governance
**Definition** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
Knowledge Governance is the computational process that isolates, audits, and integrates incremental knowledge updates to strictly preserve the global consistency, traceability, and stability of the knowledge base over time.

**Distinctions** [Validated]:
- **Learning (Acquisition)**: Parses instruction and generates a structural representation (e.g., KRS-001 graphs or gradient targets).
- **Reasoning**: Operates over the *existing* validated knowledge graph to deduce or explain.
- **Memory**: The substrate where knowledge is persisted.
- **Governance**: The gatekeeper *between* Learning and Memory. It ensures what was learned is safe to store globally without corrupting prior knowledge or introducing logical paradoxes.

---

## 3. Knowledge Lifecycle Model
Knowledge must not move from Instruction directly to Validated Global State. 

**Lifecycle States** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
1. **PROVISIONAL**: Knowledge has been acquired by the learning runtime but is isolated. It is not yet accessible for global reasoning or composition.
2. **AUDITED**: The provisional knowledge has been checked against global invariants (e.g., disjointness, contradiction checks).
3. **CONSOLIDATED**: The knowledge is safely integrated into the global memory substrates ($S_{episodic}$ and $S_{semantic}$). For $S_{semantic}$, this implies batch processing over a historical buffer rather than isolated gradient steps.
4. **VALIDATED**: The knowledge has been successfully utilized in downstream reasoning or transfer tasks without causing failures.
5. **DEPRECATED**: The knowledge has been explicitly falsified, superseded, or rejected due to low source reliability. It is retained for provenance but masked from reasoning.

---

## 4. Governance Operation Taxonomy
To advance knowledge through its lifecycle, the following computational operations are required.

**Operations** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
- `PROPOSE(K_new)`: Moves learned knowledge into the `PROVISIONAL` state. Isolates it from the global graph.
- `AUDIT(K_new, K_global, Invariants)`: A synchronous read-only global operation verifying that `K_new` does not violate invariants defined in $S_{invariants}$.
- `CONSOLIDATE(K_buffer)`: An asynchronous or batch operation that promotes `PROVISIONAL` knowledge to `CONSOLIDATED` memory, buffering it alongside historical knowledge to prevent catastrophic interference.
- `SUPERSEDE(K_old, K_new)`: Explicitly replaces older knowledge when higher-reliability knowledge contradicts it, moving `K_old` to `DEPRECATED`.
- `DEPRECATE(K_target)`: Masks a node or edge from active reasoning while preserving its source provenance.
- `CLARIFY(K_conflict)`: Halts integration and escalates to the Mentor when an `AUDIT` fails and no clear resolution exists based on source reliability.

---

## 5. Governance Invariants
For ACA to remain stable over a lifelong timeline, the Governance layer must uphold these absolute invariants.

**Invariants** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
- **INV-G1 (Global Consistency)**: No knowledge enters the `CONSOLIDATED` state if it violates a known disjointness or logical constraint currently in $S_{invariants}$.
- **INV-G2 (Stability)**: Promotion to $S_{semantic}$ must not degrade the baseline recall of historical `VALIDATED` knowledge by more than a configurable threshold $\epsilon$.
- **INV-G3 (Traceability)**: Every node and edge in $S_{episodic}$ must preserve exact source attribution, temporal ingress data, and baseline trust scores.

---

## 6. Conflict Taxonomy & Source Reliability
When `AUDIT` detects a contradiction between $K_{new}$ and $K_{old}$, resolution depends entirely on Source Reliability ($R \in [0.1, 1.0]$) and Conflict Class.

**Conflict Classes** [Reasoned Hypothesis]:
1. **High Trust Conflict ($R_{new} > R_{old}$)**: 
   - *Example*: A new textbook contradicts a previously trusted mentor.
   - *Resolution*: Apply `SUPERSEDE(K_old, K_new)`.
2. **Low Trust Conflict ($R_{new} < R_{old}$)**:
   - *Example*: An adversarial actor contradicts a known scientific fact.
   - *Resolution*: Apply `DEPRECATE(K_new)` immediately. Log the attempt.
3. **Equally Trusted Conflict ($R_{new} \approx R_{old}$)**:
   - *Example*: Two equally trusted experts disagree.
   - *Resolution*: Apply `CLARIFY`. Suspend $K_{new}$ in `PROVISIONAL` state until the mentor resolves the conflict.

Source reliability allows ACA to *understand* what it is being taught (in the `PROVISIONAL` state) without *accepting* it (into the `CONSOLIDATED` state), decoupling comprehension from belief.

---

## 7. Unified Analysis of EXP-027 and EXP-031
Both failures are elegantly solved by implementing Governance.

**Resolving EXP-027 (Contradictory Knowledge)**:
Under KGP-001, when the lesson "Whales are fish" is parsed, it enters the `PROVISIONAL` state. The `AUDIT` operation checks it against the global knowledge base. If $S_{invariants}$ contains `DisjointConstraint(Mammal, Fish)`, the audit fails. Since both lessons came from the same mentor (Equal Trust), Governance triggers `CLARIFY` instead of blindly accumulating both labels.

**Resolving EXP-031 (Long-Term Continual Learning)**:
Under KGP-001, isolated pre-eviction gradient steps are prohibited. Instead, the `PROPOSE` operation queues single-item updates into a `PROVISIONAL` buffer. The `CONSOLIDATE` operation later interleaves these new items with historical `VALIDATED` examples (a replay buffer) before performing gradient updates to $S_{semantic}$. This upholds **INV-G2**, preventing the catastrophic weight overwrites observed in EXP-031.

## 8. Architectural Impact Assessment
**Required Modifications** [Open Question]:
To implement KGP-001, future runtime iterations must introduce:
1. **GovernanceController**: A new module intercepting the flow between `lesson.py` (Learning) and `krs.py` (Memory).
2. **Provisional Buffer**: Memory space designated for un-audited graphs and un-consolidated semantic updates.
3. **DisjointConstraints**: Explicit representation of mutual exclusivity in $S_{invariants}$.
4. **Replay Buffer**: Historical sampler for the `CONSOLIDATE` operation to protect $S_{semantic}$.

*Note: In accordance with experimental discipline, these mechanisms are currently proposed, not implemented.*
